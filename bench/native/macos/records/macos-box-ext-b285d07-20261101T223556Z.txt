What this shows:
- 1 member, armed, under the ceiling-pace rule: overshoot 0.0 MiB every pass. The kernel SIGKILLs the one
  member at the whole bound (the `_ext` fatal per-process cap), so the box is bounded with no overshoot
  without the watcher polling hard. This is the native plan step 1 extension's win over the base
  (records/macos-box-ec3d08b-*, where 1 member overshot 0.2-4.1 MiB under the polling rule alone).
- Watcher CPU held at its bound is ~0.1% of a core, not the base's 12-15%: a fully-armed one-member box
  is read at the 100 ms ceiling (the kernel bounds it), not the 250 us floor. Well under criterion 3's 1%.
- More than one member per core (14, 28) still spikes (8-167 MiB): each member is capped at the *whole*
  bound, not its share, so the per-process cap doesn't bound the footprint *sum* of many members; the
  polling watcher does, and under 2x-core memory-bandwidth saturation it is descheduled and overshoots
  (as the base recorded). Step 2's root re-apportioning (per-member shares summing to the bound) owns
  the multi-member sum; this extension does not change it.
